01 — Brand at a Glance

A clinical touch for digital work.

Scout Content Studio builds websites, applications, and marketing for behavioral health practices — led by a practicing therapist who also writes the code.

Who we are. A small studio that builds, writes, and ships digital work for therapists and behavioral health practices. The work is grounded in real clinical understanding, not generic marketing playbooks.

Who we serve. Individual therapists, practice owners, group practices, and behavioral health organizations who need useful infrastructure — clearer websites, calmer intake, accurate search presence — not louder marketing.

What we stand for. Clarity over hype. Privacy and trust over pressure. A clear next step over a hard sell. The brand should feel like a calm, capable practitioner — quietly confident and genuinely helpful.

One word. Grounded.

The three pillars below run through every page of this document: the visual identity (logo, color, type, imagery) and the verbal identity (voice and tone) should always work together to make a practice feel understood.

02 — Logo

The wordmark

The identity is a high-contrast serif wordmark — SCOUT — set in Playfair Display, paired with a hairline rule and the descriptor CONTENT STUDIO in wide-tracked Jost. The serif carries warmth and editorial credibility; the spaced sans descriptor keeps it modern and calm.

Clear space & minimum size

Keep clear space around the lockup equal to the cap-height of the “S” on all sides. Never crowd it with text, rules, or imagery. Minimum legible width: 120px for the full lockup on screen. Below that, drop the descriptor and use the “S” monogram.

04 — Typography

Type system

Three open-source typefaces, each with a clear job. A high-contrast serif for presence, a soft serif for warmth, and a geometric sans for clarity in body and interface.

05 — Voice & Tone

How Scout speaks

Plain, warm, and unhurried — the way a good clinician explains something. Confident without pressure. Specific without jargon. The reader should feel understood and given a clear next step.

GroundedCalmPlain-spokenReassuringSpecific
1
Lead with the problem, not the pitch. “Start with the problem in front of you.” Meet people where they’re stuck before describing services.
2
No hype, no pressure. “Useful infrastructure, not louder marketing.” Avoid urgency, superlatives, and manipulative CTAs.
3
Protect trust and privacy. Name boundaries plainly — e.g. “Please do not send client names, diagnoses, or protected health information.”
4
Short sentences, clear next step. Always leave the reader knowing exactly what happens next.

06 — Imagery

Imagery & texture

Soft, abstract, atmospheric. Think watercolor oceans and light — quiet movement in muted blues and warm golds, never literal stock photos of therapy or people in distress. Imagery sets a calm, spacious mood; it should recede behind the type, not compete with it.

Do

  • Use soft, abstract watercolor / light textures
  • Keep tones muted: hazy blue, sage, warm gold
  • Leave generous negative space
  • Add a navy scrim when type sits over imagery

Don't

  • Use clichéd therapy or sad-person stock photos
  • Use saturated, high-energy, or busy images
  • Show identifiable clients or clinical detail
  • Let imagery overpower the headline

Standards

  • Body text ≥ 16px web · ≥ 14px mobile · captions ≥ 12px
  • Tap targets ≥ 44×44px (iOS) / 48dp (Android)
  • Form inputs ≥ 16px to prevent iOS auto-zoom
  • Provide prefers-reduced-motion fallbacks

Notes

  • Sage buttons use navy labels, not ivory
  • Never rely on color alone to convey meaning
  • Playfair & Cormorant for display only — Jost for UI
  • Icons must read clearly at 24px
